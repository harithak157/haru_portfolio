Original Tamil Poem

அரியது கேட்கின் வரிவடி வேலோய்!
அரிதரிது மானிடர் ஆதல் அரிது;
மானிடர் ஆயினும் கூன்குருடு செவிடு
பேடு நீங்கிப் பிறத்தல் அரிது;
பேடு நீங்கிப் பிறந்த காலையும்
ஞானமும் கல்வியும் நயத்தல் அரிது;
ஞானமும் கல்வியும் நயந்த காலையும்
தானமும் தவமும் தான்செயல் அரிது;
தானமும் தவமும் தான்செய்வ ராயின்
வானவர் நாடு வழிதிறந் திடுமே.

Meaning of the Poem

This philosophical poem by Avvaiyar describes the extreme rarity of a truly meaningful human life. It outlines a step-by-step progression: It is rare to be born human; even rarer to be born without physical defects; rarer still to acquire wisdom and education; and even rarer to actually practice charity and penance. However, the poet concludes that if one does perform these good deeds, the gates of heaven will open for them.

Original Source

  • Author: Avvaiyar (ஔவையார்)
  • Work: Moodhurai (மூதுரை)
  • Era: Medieval Tamil Literature

Translation Comparison

LLM English Translation

Rare is it to listen, O wielder of the striped spear!
Rare, extremely rare is it to be born as a human;
Even if born human, rare is it to be born
Without being a hunchback, blind, deaf, or disabled;
Even if born without these defects,
Rare is it to attain wisdom and education;
Even if one attains wisdom and education,
Rare is it to perform charity and penance oneself;
If one does perform charity and penance,
The gates of the heavenly realm will open wide.

Translation from Another Source

Source: Classical Tamil Scholar

Hearken, O Lord with the striped spear!
It is exceedingly rare to be born a human;
Even if human, rare to be free from hunchback, blindness, deafness, and impotence;
Even if born free from these, rare is the desire for wisdom and learning;
Even if one desires wisdom and learning, rare is the act of charity and penance;
If one does perform charity and penance, the gates of heaven open.

Observed Differences

The LLM translation provides a clearer, modern explanation for the archaic terms (translating "பேடு" as "disabled" broadly, and explicitly stating "heavenly realm"). The human translation uses older phrasing like "impotence" for "பேடு" and "hearken" for "கேட்கின்," preserving a slightly more formal, traditional tone that matches the era of the original text.
